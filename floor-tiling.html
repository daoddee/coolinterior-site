Your questions, answered.

How many floor tiles should I order?

Measure the floor and tile dimensions, then use our tile quantity calculator as a planning guide. Cut patterns, breakages and room shape affect the allowance; confirm the final order with your installer.

Can tiles be fitted over underfloor heating?

Compatibility depends on the heating system, substrate, tile and installation products. Share the system details so the requirements, commissioning and work sequence can be reviewed before fitting.

How long will the work take?

Timing depends on the area, preparation, tile format, cuts and product curing requirements. Share the scope so we can discuss a realistic sequence and availability before work starts.

Practical guidance · Cool Interior

A floor needs more than a good-looking tile.

Clear information helps you choose a finish that suits your home. These are starting points for a project conversation; the final specification and schedule follow assessment of your space.

Match the floor to your household

Floor-rated porcelain or ceramic may suit kitchens, hallways and living areas, depending on the product. Consider wear, ease of cleaning, footwear, wet entrances and visual continuity between rooms. Natural stone needs material-specific care. No finish should be described as slip-proof.

Backgrounds, heating and movement

Existing timber, screed and concrete need different assessments. Check stability, moisture, finished levels and any underfloor-heating requirements before choosing the installation system. Movement joints and compatible products help accommodate the building’s behaviour. Heating commissioning and restart must follow the system and fixing-product instructions.

When can the room be used again?

A modest, straightforward room may involve around 2–4 fitting days as an illustrative planning allowance. Removal, moisture issues, levelling, complex cuts and adjoining rooms can add time. Walking, heavy furniture and heating may each have different restart times; we discuss them against the actual product requirements.
