Planning Commercial Tile Replacement in London

By Cool Interior · 21 October 2026

In this guide

A cracked tile at an entrance or a worn reception floor affects a space that staff and visitors use throughout the day. Replacing it requires more than selecting an attractive finish. A useful commercial tiling brief also explains how the area operates and what the business needs during installation.

For London offices, hospitality spaces and managed properties, early planning gives contractors a clearer basis for pricing and helps the client understand the effect of the work on normal activity.

Establish why the existing floor needs attention

Record the damaged locations and the operational problem they create. Are individual tiles loose, is cleaning difficult, or does the finish no longer suit a refurbishment? Share previous repair records and any available product information.

A limited defect may call for a different approach from a wider change of use or layout. Ask for the proposed scope to identify the assessed problem and the areas included. This helps procurement teams compare quotations without assuming every contractor has allowed for the same work.

Select flooring around its use

HSE advises that workplace flooring should be suitable for the activity taking place and highlights the relevance of slip resistance where contamination is expected. Product selection therefore needs to reflect the location and conditions. See HSE's workplace flooring guidance.

Describe foot traffic, entrances exposed to rain, cleaning routines and any equipment that moves across the floor. Request the supplier's relevant performance and care information. An appropriate product for a dry office wall should not automatically become the specification for a busy entrance floor.

Agree access and the sequence of work

Provide permitted working hours, loading arrangements, lift restrictions and the routes available for materials and waste. Identify areas that must remain accessible and ask whether the proposed sequence can accommodate them.

If phased work is possible, establish the boundaries, dependencies and handover points. Do not assume an area can remain open or that evening installation is available before this has been assessed and agreed. Drying and curing requirements also affect when a completed area can return to service.

Compare quotations against one clear brief

State the preparation, removal and finishing requirements you want priced, or identify where investigation is still needed. Confirm responsibility for tile supply, adhesive, grout, trims and waste handling. Include thresholds and junctions so these are considered alongside the main floor area.

Ask how concealed damage, scope changes and additional costs will be documented. The client should understand both the agreed price and the process for resolving information that becomes available during the work.

Plan the appearance and future maintenance

Use sample areas to consider tile and grout together under the site's lighting. Discuss the layout around reception desks, doorways and adjoining finishes. Request appropriate care instructions and confirm who will receive them at handover.

For a managed property, keep the final product references and any relevant installation information with the maintenance records. Ask whether retaining spare tiles is sensible for the agreed layout and future repairs.

Common questions about commercial tiling

Can installation happen while the premises remain occupied? It depends on the site, agreed working arrangements and safe segregation. Share operational restrictions before the programme is developed.

Can damaged tiles be replaced individually? A local replacement may be practical if the installation and matching materials permit it. The defect and surrounding condition need assessment.

What information helps with a commercial quotation? Send drawings or floor areas, photographs, the site postcode, access details, preferred materials and the required completion window.
