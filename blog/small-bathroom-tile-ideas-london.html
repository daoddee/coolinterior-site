A small bathroom can still feel spacious, comfortable and beautifully finished. The key is choosing tiles that work with the room’s proportions, lighting and layout.

Whether you’re renovating a compact en suite, updating a narrow bathroom in a London terrace or refreshing a flat with limited natural light, thoughtful tiling can make the space feel calmer and more open.

At CoolInterior, we believe the best results come from considering the whole room, from tile size and grout colour to the cuts around windows, corners and fittings.

1. Choose Light Colours Without Making Everything White

White tiles are a classic choice, but they’re not the only option for a small bathroom. Warm ivory, pale beige, soft grey and muted stone shades can create a light, welcoming appearance without making the room feel stark.

Before ordering, compare samples inside your bathroom during the day and with the lights switched on. A tile that looks warm in a showroom may appear cooler in a room with little daylight.

Design idea: Pair warm ivory wall tiles with a pale stone-effect floor and simple brushed metal fittings.

2. Consider Larger Tiles for a Calmer Finish

Small bathrooms don’t automatically need small tiles. Larger tiles can reduce the number of visible grout lines, creating a less fragmented appearance. However, the best size depends on the room’s dimensions and where the cuts will fall.

A medium-format tile with balanced cuts can look better than an oversized tile squeezed around fixtures. Before choosing, ask your installer to assess the layout around the bath, shower, doorway and window.

Our advice: Choose a tile size that suits the room, rather than simply buying the largest available.

3. Use Grout That Complements the Tiles

Grout colour changes how strongly the tile pattern stands out. A colour close to the tile shade allows the joints to blend into the surface. Contrasting grout emphasises each tile, which can create an attractive feature but also a busier appearance.

For a softer finish, consider ivory tiles with warm off-white grout, beige tiles with a complementary sand shade, or pale grey tiles with light grey grout.

Grout joints must still suit the tile and installation system. A continuous appearance should never depend on fitting tiles without appropriate joints.

4. Connect the Wall and Floor Colours

Using related colours across the walls and floor can make the room feel more cohesive. You don’t need identical tiles on every surface. A pale limestone-effect floor, for example, can work beautifully with warm neutral walls.

If you choose one tile range for both surfaces, confirm that the specific products are suitable for their intended use. A wall tile is not automatically suitable for flooring.

Design idea: Use a coordinated range with a suitable floor finish and a smoother wall finish.

5. Use Marble Effect Without Overwhelming the Room

Marble-effect porcelain can introduce character and a luxurious appearance to a compact bathroom. For a quieter scheme, choose a light background with gentle veining. Strong patterns can also work, but they benefit from careful placement.

Consider using a more expressive marble effect on the shower wall or behind the basin, with simpler finishes elsewhere. Discuss the arrangement before installation, especially where the design includes a prominent vein or directional pattern.

6. Choose One Main Feature

A small bathroom can include colour and pattern without feeling crowded. The trick is to give one feature room to stand out: a patterned floor beneath plain walls, a coloured wall behind the basin, a textured shower wall or a decorative niche within a simpler tiled surface.

Combining several bold finishes can make the room feel visually busy. Start with your favourite feature and build the rest of the scheme around it.

7. Explore Vertical and Horizontal Layouts

Rectangular tiles can influence how you read a room’s proportions. A vertical arrangement draws attention upwards and may suit a bathroom where you want to emphasise height. A horizontal layout can emphasise the width of a wall.

Neither is a guaranteed solution. Windows, fittings and the room’s shape all affect the result. Ask to compare a few layout options before installation, including where narrow cuts would appear.

8. Plan Tiles Around Mirrors and Lighting

Tiles should work with the bathroom’s lighting, rather than being chosen in isolation. Glossy wall tiles can reflect light, while matt finishes offer a softer appearance. A well-positioned mirror can also help distribute available light and create a greater sense of depth.

Check samples under your intended lighting. Strong reflections may highlight surface variation, while heavily textured tiles can cast shadows. For floors, prioritise a product suitable for bathroom use and the expected wet conditions.

9. Keep Niches, Corners and Edges Neat

In a compact bathroom, finishing details are easy to notice. Balanced cuts, aligned joints and carefully planned edges can make the entire installation feel more considered.

Discuss tile alignment around the window, cuts beside the bath and shower, the position and size of any niche, trims or mitred external corners, and transitions at the doorway before work begins.

A recessed niche can provide useful storage, but its location and construction need checking before installation. It also needs appropriate waterproofing.

10. Make Preparation Part of the Design

Even attractive tiles can disappoint when the surface beneath them is unsuitable. Uneven backgrounds can affect alignment and make installation more difficult, particularly with larger tiles.

Preparation may involve repairs, suitable boarding, levelling and waterproofing where required. Tiles and grout alone should not be treated as a complete waterproofing system.

At quotation stage, ask what preparation is included and how any hidden problems will be handled.

Frequently Asked Questions

What colour tiles make a small bathroom look bigger?

Light shades such as ivory, cream, pale grey and soft beige can help create an open appearance. The effect also depends on lighting, grout colour and how much contrast you introduce.

Are large tiles suitable for a small bathroom?

Yes, provided the tile size and layout suit the room. Check the proposed cuts, surface preparation and access before ordering.

Can I use dark tiles in a small bathroom?

Yes. Dark tiles can create a rich, intimate atmosphere. If your priority is a brighter appearance, consider using them as a focused feature alongside lighter surfaces.

Should bathroom wall and floor tiles match?

They don’t have to match. Coordinating colours can create a cohesive scheme while allowing different finishes for walls and floors.

How much does it cost to tile a small bathroom in London?

The price depends on the tiled area, materials, preparation and installation details. Small rooms can still involve substantial cutting around fittings, windows and niches. Request a written quotation covering the installation scope, preparation, removal and disposal, and any waterproofing required.

A successful small bathroom isn’t about following one rule. It’s about bringing the tiles, layout, lighting and finishing details together.
