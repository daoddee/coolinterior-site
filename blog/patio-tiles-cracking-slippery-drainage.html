Planning Better Patio Tiles for London Gardens

By Suhib Al Wadi · 21 October 2026

In this guide

A patio should be a space you want to use. Cracked tiles, water collecting near a doorway or steps that feel slippery can quickly take away that enjoyment.

Before replacing patio tiles, consider how the whole outdoor area works. The tile choice, supporting construction, water management and transitions at steps or doors all deserve a place in the installation brief.

Describe the problem in its conditions

Photograph the terrace as a whole and the areas you are concerned about. If water collects after rain, record where it remains. Share whether the problem is confined to a few tiles or appears across the patio, and whether previous repairs have been carried out.

Include door thresholds, changes in level and access to the garden in the photographs. These details help a contractor understand the project before discussing the assessment and quotation.

Ask what is causing the outdoor tiles to fail

The Tile Association highlights movement provision and water management as important considerations in external tiling. Outdoor exposure needs to be addressed in the installation design. Replacing the visible tiles without reviewing the supporting system may leave the same practical problem. Read the association's external floor tiling guidance.

Ask the contractor to explain which findings require corrective work and how the proposed installation responds to them. A clear explanation makes it easier to assess the value of preparation that will be hidden once the terrace is finished.

Select a product for the outdoor location

Check the manufacturer's stated applications for the exact tile. Ask about exterior suitability, local exposure, wet conditions and cleaning requirements. A showroom appearance or the word porcelain alone does not settle those questions.

Discuss the finish beside the materials around it, including brickwork, planting areas and any existing steps. If you want continuity between an indoor room and the garden, establish whether different products or finishes are needed for each setting.

Consider grip and cleaning together

HSE explains that contamination such as water can affect slip risk on smooth floors. For a patio, that makes the expected wet conditions and maintenance relevant to the material discussion. Supplier performance information should be considered alongside how the surface will be used and cared for. Read HSE's guidance on contamination.

Ask how the selected surface can be cleaned using the manufacturer's recommended approach. Share any particular needs around children, mobility, pets or frequently used routes so these inform the brief.

Plan the steps and edges as part of the terrace

Review where the tiles finish, how step edges will be treated and how the finished levels relate to doors. Ask the installer to include these details in the scope rather than pricing only the open area.

Our Finchley terrace and stair project shows an outdoor layout with coordinated steps. Use it as a reference for discussing proportions and fitting details. Your own site conditions determine the required installation approach.

What to include in a patio tiling survey

Plan the outdoor installation as a complete system. A new surface should respond to the existing defects and the way water moves through the space.

  • Photograph standing water after rain and show the route towards existing drainage points.
  • Include doors, air bricks, walls, steps and changes in level in the brief so critical junctions can be assessed.
  • Provide the exact tile reference and ask for evidence of suitability for the intended exterior application.
  • Explain who uses the route and any mobility needs, furniture loads or regular cleaning challenges.
  • Describe garden access, material storage and the route for removing waste through the property.

Ask the questions that prevent a repeat problem

Before accepting a quote, ask: What is causing the cracking or water collection, and what will change underneath the new tiles? The response should explain the assessed background, water-management approach, movement provision and edge details appropriate to the site.

Request product information relevant to wet use rather than relying on a generic “anti-slip” description. No tile removes every slip risk: water, contamination, footwear and maintenance still matter. Discuss the intended conditions with the supplier and installer.

For timing, allow the programme to account for weather-sensitive work, background preparation and the chosen products' curing conditions. Ask when the garden route will be unavailable and when furniture can return. Agree how delays or unexpected findings will be communicated.

Retain suitable existing construction where assessment supports it, and agree quantities before ordering. Correcting the cause of failure can avoid repeated removal, replacement costs and material waste.

Common questions about outdoor tile replacement

Can indoor tiles be used on a patio? Only use a product that the manufacturer confirms is suitable for the intended external application.

Will new tiles solve standing water? Not automatically. The levels and route for water need assessment before the replacement is specified.

Can you price the patio from its area alone? Measurements help, but access, the existing installation, preparation, steps and edge details also affect the scope.
