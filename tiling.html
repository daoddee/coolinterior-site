Practical guidance · Cool Interior

Which tiles suit the way you live?

The best tile is the one that suits the room, the background it is fixed to and the care you want to give it. We help London homeowners compare appearance with everyday use before agreeing an installation.

Ceramic: colour and practical wall finishes

Ceramic tiles offer a wide choice of colours, glazes and shapes for bathroom walls and kitchen splashbacks. Some are suitable for floors; others are wall-only. We check the manufacturer’s intended use rather than choosing by appearance alone. A smaller tile can follow a detailed layout, but creates more grout joints to maintain.

Porcelain: busy floors and coordinated spaces

Porcelain is a low-water-absorption ceramic tile. A suitably specified product can be a practical option for busy kitchens, hallways and bathroom floors. Porcelain is not automatically slip-resistant or suitable outdoors: finish, frost suitability and the complete installation system still matter. Explore porcelain installation.

Marble: natural character with considered care

Natural marble offers individual veining and variation for surfaces where character matters. It needs a different care routine from glazed ceramic or porcelain: acidic spills and cleaners can etch its surface. If easy maintenance is your priority, compare marble with a suitable marble-effect porcelain sample. Explore marble services.

Mosaic and patterned tiles: detail and definition

Mosaic, geometric and patterned layouts can define a shower area, splashback or feature floor. More joints mean more cleaning, and sheet-mounted products need careful alignment. A mosaic is not automatically a safe shower-floor tile; confirm the product’s suitability and drainage requirements.

Large-format tiles: fewer joints, more planning

Larger tiles can create a calm, continuous appearance. They demand careful background assessment, handling, cutting and setting out. Plan access through your property and cuts around windows, corners and fittings before ordering. A large tile does not correct an uneven floor.

Choosing for your household

For a busy family kitchen, start with cleaning, wear and floor suitability. For a bathroom, consider wet use, drainage and safe movement. For an outdoor terrace, start with exterior suitability, falls and weather exposure. For a statement interior, compare samples in your actual light alongside cabinets, paint and grout.

Practical guidance · Cool Interior

From your first photos to the final details.

Good tiling starts before the first tile is fitted. Our family business brings a father’s 30-year career in the trade together with his graduate engineer son’s interest in materials, design and better homes. This is how we plan a project with you.

1. Understand your space

Send your postcode, photographs, approximate dimensions and the areas you want tiled. Tell us about your budget, preferred finish, access and whether the room must remain usable. We discuss the scope and arrange further assessment where needed before confirming the work.

2. Agree the specification and responsibilities

We confirm the proposed surfaces, tile sizes, layout, trims, grout and preparation requirements. The quote should make clear what is included, who supplies materials and what sits outside the tiling scope. Hidden damage or changes need a discussion before additional work proceeds.

3. Coordinate the people and sequence

We agree a point of contact and plan the tiling sequence with you and any other trades involved. Plumbing, electrical work, waterproofing, stone fabrication and decorating have different dependencies. We clarify who is responsible for each task and when surfaces must be ready; separate specialist work is agreed in the scope.

4. Prepare, set out and install

Preparation is chosen for the existing background and specified products. We discuss focal points, balanced cuts, junctions and movement accommodation before fitting. Wet areas need an appropriate waterproofing system: tiles and grout alone are not a substitute for waterproofing.

5. Allow time for curing and checks

Adhesive, grout, waterproofing and sealants have their own drying or curing requirements. We plan the next task around the product instructions and site conditions, rather than promising that every room can be used the next day. Layout changes and unforeseen preparation can affect the programme.

6. Handover and looking after your finish

At handover, review the finish and any outstanding details together. Ask when the surface can be walked on, cleaned or exposed to water, and which care products suit your tiles or stone. Keep spare tiles and product information for future repairs.

Practical guidance · Cool Interior

Made to last. Planned with less waste.

Sustainability is a series of practical choices, not a label that applies to every tile. Our aim is to help clients choose suitable, lasting surfaces and avoid unnecessary replacement. The right answer depends on the existing property, product information and budget.

Durability before a quick cosmetic fix

A suitable tile installed on a properly prepared background can serve a home for decades. There is no single guaranteed lifespan: movement, water exposure, workmanship, use and maintenance all matter. Grout, seals and flexible joints may need attention sooner than the tiles. Addressing the cause of a problem is better than covering it.

Measure carefully and keep useful spares

Plan quantities around the layout, tile size, cuts and pack sizes. Use our tile calculator as a starting estimate, then confirm the order against the design. Avoid excessive over-ordering while allowing enough for cuts and future repairs. Keep labelled spare tiles from the same batch.

Compare the whole material story

Ask suppliers about manufacturing, recycled content, environmental product declarations, transport and packaging where available. Natural stone and manufactured tiles have different impacts; neither is automatically the greener choice. Retaining a sound existing finish or repairing a limited area can be worth considering if technically suitable.

Further reading: The Tile Association: tile durability · TTA: sustainability · Natural Stone Institute: stone care. Product-specific instructions take precedence for your installation.
