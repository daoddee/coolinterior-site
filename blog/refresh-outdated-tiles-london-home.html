Refreshing Dated Tiles in Your London Home

By Suhib Al Wadi · 21 October 2026

In this guide

You can have a perfectly usable bathroom and still dislike walking into it. An unwanted colour, a busy border or floor tiles that clash with a new kitchen can make the whole space feel unfinished.

Updating outdated bathroom tiles or a kitchen splashback gives you an opportunity to make the room feel more like your home. A clear brief helps you achieve that change while keeping the work proportionate to your priorities and budget.

Start with what bothers you about the existing room

Write down the specific features you want to change. Is it the tile colour, the pattern, the joint layout or the way different finishes meet? Then identify what you still like, such as the kitchen cabinets, bath or room arrangement.

This creates a more useful starting point than choosing a fashionable tile and trying to make every other decision fit around it. It also helps your contractor understand whether you want a limited refresh or a wider renovation.

Compare three levels of change

For a focused refresh, consider whether changing a splashback or another clearly defined area would achieve your main goal. For a coordinated update, discuss connected wall and floor finishes. For a larger renovation, establish which other trades are involved and agree the tiling sequence around their work.

The condition of existing surfaces influences what can be retained. Ask for that assessment before assuming the smallest visible change will be the simplest installation.

Choose a complete combination of finishes

Review the tile beside a grout sample and the materials that will remain in the room. Photograph the combination in daylight and under the lighting you normally use. Screen images are useful for gathering ideas, but physical samples give you a better basis for agreeing the finish.

If you like veined surfaces, compare the pattern and maintenance requirements of marble installation with porcelain tiling. Discuss how the exact product will be used before ordering.

Make a newly purchased property feel like yours

If you have just moved, prioritise the areas you use most and share any planned dates for decorating, fitting a kitchen or moving furniture. Let the installer know whether the property will be occupied during the work.

Create a short list of essential decisions and another of optional changes. Agree the essential scope first, then ask how the optional work affects price and timing. This makes it easier to manage a refresh without letting every attractive idea expand the project.

Use local projects to explain the look you want

Our Willesden Green bathroom project shows patterned flooring and contrasting surfaces. Use the photographs to discuss layout, colour relationships and edges. A project image can explain your preferences more clearly than a broad description such as modern or luxurious.

For a kitchen update, explore our kitchen tiling service. Share photographs of your existing units and worktop so the new tiles can be considered in context.

Choose the smallest change that solves the problem

Keep what works and define what needs to change. This helps control the budget and reduces unnecessary strip-out.

  • Sound tiles, tired appearance: first assess whether appropriate cleaning or joint repairs would achieve enough improvement.
  • A dated splashback or border: discuss a defined replacement area and how its edges will meet retained finishes.
  • A whole room that feels disconnected: review the floor, walls, fittings and lighting together before pricing individual changes.
  • Loose tiles, damp or recurring cracks: investigate the defect before treating the job as a decorative update.

Make decisions before ordering materials

Create a one-page brief showing the retained finishes, tile samples, preferred grout, edge details and the areas included. Agree the layout around focal points such as a basin, kitchen window or entrance. Confirm quantities with the installer, including cutting allowance, rather than buying only the measured surface area.

For tiling over existing tiles, ask specifically about the existing bond, background suitability, additional height, door clearance and junctions. Covering the old finish is not automatically the least disruptive or best-value option.

Where other trades are involved, agree who removes and reinstates fittings and when surfaces will be ready for tiling. Keep tile delivery, preparation, installation and return-to-use dates separate. Tell us if the home will remain occupied so access and protection can be discussed before booking.

Use our tiling cost and quotation guide to compare inclusions. Investing in a suitable specification matters more than following a trend that does not suit your home.

Common questions about updating old tiles

Can I replace the tiles without replacing the whole bathroom? A defined tiling scope may be possible. The feasibility depends on the existing condition, access and how the tiles meet the fittings.

Can new tiles be installed over old ones? Sometimes, subject to assessment. Existing adhesion, background suitability and the effect of additional thickness all need consideration.

Will new tiles increase my property's value? A refreshed finish may improve presentation, but there is no guaranteed return. Plan around suitability, workmanship, your budget and how you intend to use the property.
