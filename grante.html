Practical guidance · Cool Interior

Living with marble: beauty, specification and care.

Clear information helps you choose a finish that suits your home. These are starting points for a project conversation; the final specification and schedule follow assessment of your space.

When is marble a good fit?

Marble suits clients who value natural variation and accept a more considered care routine. Choose the actual slab or samples and discuss veining, edges, joins and support. For a busy kitchen where acidic spills are frequent, compare alternatives before committing. A marble-effect porcelain tile is a different product with a different installation process.

Who measures, fabricates and installs?

Stone worktops, islands and vanities may require specialist templating, fabrication, handling and installation. We clarify who is responsible for each part of the agreed scope, along with sink and tap cut-outs, cabinet readiness and plumbing connections. Do not assume a tiling quotation includes slab fabrication or other trades.

How long does marble work take?

The overall lead time includes selection, measurement or templating, fabrication scheduling and installation. It cannot reliably be described as a single fitting-day estimate. Final site measurements depend on the supporting units being ready; confirm supplier availability and the sequence before booking linked trades.

Care that protects your investment

Use cleaning products approved for the specific stone. Acidic products such as vinegar or lemon-based cleaners can etch marble. An appropriate impregnating sealer may reduce staining but does not make marble stain-proof or protect it from acid etching. Follow the supplier’s care and resealing guidance.

How long can marble last?

Natural stone can remain useful for decades when appropriately specified, supported and maintained. Wear, staining and surface changes depend on use and care. Ask whether repair or professional refinishing is suitable before replacing an existing surface; the answer depends on the stone and condition.

Design with less avoidable waste

Agree slab layouts and visible joins before fabrication, and discuss whether suitable remnants could serve smaller areas. Natural variation is part of the design. Product origin, transport, fabrication and long-term use all contribute to environmental impact; natural stone is not automatically a low-impact choice.
