Leaking Shower Tiles and Choosing the Right Repair

By Suhib Al Wadi · 21 October 2026

Water marks near a shower, damp around the enclosure or grout that keeps breaking down can make a bathroom difficult to trust. Before paying for new silicone or replacement tiles, establish where the water is coming from. The right repair depends on whether the defect is in a joint, the plumbing, the fittings or the construction behind the tiled surface.

For London homeowners planning bathroom work, that distinction can prevent paying twice for a problem that was never properly identified.

Why a tiled shower can still leak

The visible tiles are one part of the shower installation. Manufacturer BAL explains that tiles, adhesive and grout do not replace a suitable waterproofing system beneath the finish. A defect behind the tiles may therefore need a different repair from a damaged surface joint. BAL's wet-area waterproofing guidance explains this distinction.

Start by recording where the moisture appears and when it becomes noticeable. Photograph the affected area and any damage in the adjacent room. Share whether the problem happens during shower use, afterwards or independently of it. These observations help the appropriate trade plan an inspection; they are not a diagnosis on their own.

When resealing or regrouting may be appropriate

Resealing may be relevant where a flexible joint has deteriorated and the surrounding installation remains suitable. Regrouting may be considered where the grout itself is defective but the tiles and supporting construction are sound.

Ask what evidence supports the proposed repair and whether the source of the leak has been established. A cosmetic improvement should have a clear purpose. If the same problem has returned after previous work, provide that history before another repair is agreed.

When removing tiles may be necessary

Where investigation identifies damaged backing materials, loose tiles or a defective waterproofing installation, access behind the finish may be needed. The affected area determines whether a local repair is practical or a wider section should be renewed. Plumbing or drainage defects need the relevant specialist's attention.

Before removal begins, agree who is responsible for investigating the leak, repairing the background, completing waterproofing and reinstalling the tiles. An inspection may change the initial scope once concealed areas are visible. Your quotation should explain how additional work will be agreed.

Make the replacement improve the whole shower

If retiling is needed, use the opportunity to review the details you live with every day. Consider how the tile layout meets the tray, the position of trims and corners, cleaning access and the appearance of grout joints. Check that the chosen products are suitable for the location.

Browse our bathroom tiling service in London to explore the planning considerations. Our Willesden Green bathroom project shows a completed layout and fitting details; photographs of a finished room do not verify concealed waterproofing.

Plan cost and bathroom access before booking

Ask for a scope that identifies removal, background work, waterproofing responsibilities, installation materials and waste arrangements. Establish when the shower will be unavailable and when the specified products permit it to return to use. Review our tiling quotation guide before comparing prices.

What to do before arranging a shower repair

Start with investigation, then choose the repair. If shower use is causing an active leak, stop using it until the source has been assessed. Repeatedly running water to test the problem can add to the damage.

  • Record the symptoms: photograph damp patches, failed joints and affected ceilings without removing tiles yourself.
  • Check the repair history: note when the shower was installed and whether previous silicone or grout repairs helped temporarily.
  • Identify the right trade: suspected supply, waste or fitting faults need plumbing investigation; the tiling scope follows the findings.
  • Agree the decision point: ask what can be confirmed before opening up and when you will approve any additional work.

Reseal, regrout or retile: how to compare the options

Resealing targets a defective flexible joint. Regrouting renews unsuitable grout in an otherwise sound installation. Retiling provides access where the background or waterproofing needs correction. None should be selected solely because it is the cheapest visible fix.

For example, fresh grout may improve the appearance of a shower while leaving a concealed leak unresolved. Ask the contractor to explain the identified defect, the proposed remedy and any remaining uncertainty in writing.

For the programme, separate investigation, removal, background repairs, waterproofing, tiling and return to use. Product curing requirements and hidden conditions determine the dates; a small tiled area does not automatically mean a same-day repair.

Common questions about leaking shower tiles

Will new grout stop a leaking shower? It may address a grout-related defect where that is the established cause. It will not repair unrelated plumbing faults or a defective waterproofing layer.

Does a leak mean the whole bathroom needs replacing? No. The extent of the problem and the condition of the surrounding installation determine the work required.

What should I send with an enquiry? Include photographs, your postcode, the approximate affected area and details of previous repairs. A site assessment may be needed before an accurate quotation.
