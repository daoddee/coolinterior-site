How to Find a Reliable Tiler in London: 10 Things to Check

Finding a reliable tiler in London starts with knowing what to ask before you book. Attractive photographs are useful, but you also need to understand how the installer will prepare your surfaces, manage the work and explain the costs.

Whether you are updating a bathroom, fitting a kitchen splashback or planning a tiled hallway, the right questions help you compare contractors with confidence.

Before hiring a tiler, check their relevant experience, recent reviews, written quotation, insurance, preparation plan, waterproofing approach, material knowledge, schedule, payment terms and aftercare.

Here is how to assess each one.

1. Look for experience with your type of tiling project

Start by asking for examples that resemble the work you need. A kitchen splashback, patterned bathroom floor and large outdoor terrace each present different fitting challenges.

Make your brief specific. Explain the room, tile material, approximate dimensions and any features such as niches, steps or underfloor heating.

Ask to see photographs showing:

  • The complete room and close-up fitting details.
  • Cuts around corners, fittings and doorways.
  • Grout lines and where different surfaces meet.
  • Work in progress, where available.

For example, our Finchley terrace and stair tiling project shows an outdoor installation with steps and raised features. These photographs give you particular details to discuss when planning a comparable project.

Ask the tiler: “Can you show me a project involving a similar layout and tile type?”

2. Read recent reviews and check references

Look beyond the overall star rating. Read what customers say about punctuality, communication, cleanliness and how the installer handled questions.

Recent feedback about similar work is particularly useful. Where possible, ask for a previous customer’s reference and permission to contact them.

Citizens Advice recommends checking recent examples of comparable work and speaking to references.

Ask the tiler: “Do you have a recent customer who would be happy to discuss their experience?”

3. Request a clear, written quotation

Get the price and scope in writing. Check whether the document is a quotation or an estimate, and compare contractors using the same brief.

Confirm whether the price covers:

  • Removing existing tiles.
  • Preparing walls or floors.
  • Waterproofing where specified.
  • Adhesive, grout, trims and sealants.
  • Installation and finishing.
  • Waste removal.
  • VAT, where applicable.

Ask what remains uncertain and how additional work would be agreed. Citizens Advice recommends comparing written quotations and checking the work, materials and VAT treatment.

Ask the tiler: “What is excluded, and what could change the final price?”

4. Check insurance and verify claimed credentials

Ask for evidence of current public liability insurance and confirm that it is appropriate for the planned work.

If an installer advertises qualifications, training or trade association membership, ask for details you can verify. A logo alone gives you little information about its current status.

The Federation of Master Builders recommends checking active insurance and verifying contractors’ credentials before appointing them.

Ask the tiler: “Can you provide your current insurance details and evidence of any credentials you advertise?”

5. Find out how the surfaces will be prepared

Ask what the tiler needs to inspect before confirming the installation method. Existing tiles, damaged backgrounds, uneven surfaces and signs of moisture all deserve attention.

A useful explanation should identify what needs removing, repairing or assessing further. BAL’s technical guidance highlights the importance of a sound, clean and suitably prepared background before waterproofing and tiling.

For a floor, also discuss how the finished height will meet doorways and neighbouring rooms.

Ask the tiler: “What preparation have you allowed for, and what can only be confirmed after removal?”

6. Discuss waterproofing before bathroom tiling begins

When choosing a bathroom tiler in London, ask how the areas exposed to water will be protected.

Tiles and grout alone should not be treated as a complete waterproofing system. BAL explains that a tiled finish does not replace a properly waterproofed installation in showers and wet rooms.

Ask which system is proposed, where it will be applied and how corners, junctions and pipe penetrations will be addressed. The specification should suit the background and follow the selected system’s instructions.

You can explore our bathroom tiling services in London when preparing your project brief.

Ask the tiler: “What waterproofing system will you use, and which areas are included?”

7. Check their knowledge of your chosen tiles

Share the actual product details before accepting a fitting price. Tile size, thickness and material can influence handling, cutting and installation planning.

Large porcelain panels, for example, need careful consideration of access, equipment, background flatness and adhesive application. The Tile Association highlights these factors in its guidance on large-format porcelain.

Discuss your design priorities too. If you have chosen a veined tile, patterned floor or decorative border, agree how the layout will be reviewed before fixing begins.

For marble, explain which surfaces you want fitted and provide the supplier’s product information. Our marble installation page provides a starting point for that conversation.

Ask the tiler: “What does this particular tile require, and can we agree the layout before installation?”

8. Agree a realistic schedule and access arrangements

Ask for a proposed start date, installation sequence and expected completion date. Separately confirm when you can walk on the floor, use the shower or return furniture to the room.

These may be different milestones. The programme should account for the preparation and curing requirements of the products being used.

For a London property, raise practical questions early:

  • Where can the installer park and unload?
  • Are there stairs, lift restrictions or narrow access points?
  • Where will tiles and materials be stored?
  • Where can cutting take place?
  • Which areas need protection?
  • Are working hours restricted by the building?

Write these details into your brief so each contractor prices the same working conditions.

Ask the tiler: “When will installation finish, and when will the space be ready for normal use?”

9. Put payment terms and changes in writing

Before booking, agree what any deposit covers, when subsequent payments fall due and how changes will be priced.

For larger projects, payments may follow agreed stages. Keep invoices and receipts, and use a traceable payment method. The Federation of Master Builders recommends documenting payment arrangements and checking completed stages before paying.

Ask the tiler: “What are the payment stages, and how will you obtain my approval for extra work?”

10. Clarify handover, snagging and aftercare

Agree how you will inspect the finished work together and record anything requiring attention. Ask who to contact afterwards and request the terms of any workmanship cover in writing.

The Federation of Master Builders recommends clarifying guarantees and the handling of defects before work starts.

Use the handover to request product-specific guidance on cleaning, maintenance and when the newly tiled area can be used.

Ask the tiler: “What information will I receive at handover, and how should I report a concern?”

How to compare tilers without focusing only on price

When reviewing quotations, ask yourself one practical question: Can I explain exactly what each installer has included?

Consider this hypothetical example. One price covers fitting tiles to a prepared surface. Another also includes removing the existing finish, repairing the background and disposing of waste. The totals describe different jobs.

Send each contractor the same photographs, measurements and product details. Then ask them to identify assumptions and exclusions. This makes your comparison more useful and gives you a clearer basis for choosing.

Frequently asked questions

How do I find a reliable tiler near me in London?

Build a shortlist using recommendations, local project examples and customer feedback. Send each installer the same brief, then work through the ten checks above. Choose someone whose experience and proposed approach match your project.

What should I send a tiler when requesting a quote?

Send your postcode, photographs, approximate measurements and a description of the work. Include the tile product details, preferred timing and anything you know about access or the existing surfaces. Explain whether you need removal and waste disposal included.

Can a tiler quote from photographs?

Photographs can help an installer understand the initial scope. Ask whether the response is an indicative price or a final quotation, and what needs inspecting before it can be confirmed. Hidden background conditions may require further assessment.

Should I supply my own tiles?

Discuss this before placing an order. Agree who will check suitability, calculate quantities and handle deliveries or damaged items. If you already have tiles, provide their dimensions, product name and available quantity.

How can I check the quality of finished tiling?

Inspect the work with the installer against the agreed layout and specification. Look closely at cuts, joints, edges and transitions. Ask questions about anything you are uncertain about and record agreed follow-up work during the handover.
